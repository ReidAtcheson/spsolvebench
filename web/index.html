A public, trustless benchmark for solving Ax = b with large sparse A.

Five times each UTC day, a drand beacon seeds a fresh sparse linear system (223 or 226 rows) unknown until release, so every entrant on a round solves the exact same matrix on the same public clock (drand → Sigstore RFC 3161). Submit a compact proof of your solution and runtime without revealing x; short hardware and algorithm writeups are encouraged.

How a round works
  1. Wait for an official start round: 5 evenly spaced drand Quicknet rounds per day (00:00, 04:48, 09:36, 14:24, 19:12 UTC; every 5,760 rounds).
  2. Solve & commit: Derive the matrix A and RHS b from the Quicknet signature, solve Ax = b using any algorithm or hardware, and compute Ristretto255 row commitments to x.
  3. Stop the clock: Stamp the commitment digest with Sigstore Public Good’s RFC 3161 TSA before your chosen proof_round. Elapsed time runs from scheduled drand release to the signed TSA timestamp.
  4. Prove offline & submit: Once proof_round arrives, run stencil prove-beacon offline to build a compact proof.bin, sign your submission with an Ed25519 key, and POST to https://api.spsolvebench.com/submit.
Matrix generator

The competition profile (signed-three-band-backward80-v2) draws a symmetric-indefinite sparse matrix of dimension n ∈ {223 = 8,388,608, 226 = 67,108,864} with 7n − 12 nonzeros: a signed diagonal plus three off-diagonal bands per row centered at offsets 1, n1/3, and n2/3 (bounded within ±200 columns of each center, with a 1% tail). Every nonzero entry of A is +1 or −1, and each RHS entry is bi = ±1/n.

Because the matrix is indefinite with ±1 entries, direct elimination encounters frequent exact 1 − 1 = 0 cancellations, requiring pivoting or shifting plus iterative refinement to reach machine-precision backward error. Generator spec (THREE_BAND.md) ↗

Commitment & residual proof

The prover quantizes x into a shared-exponent integer vector arranged as a rectangle and commits to each row with unblinded Ristretto255 vector commitments (Hyrax-style). After proof_round supplies fresh randomness, Fiat–Shamir drives two degree-2 sumchecks plus 128 ChaCha20 subset-sum range checks that tie the squared residual norm, squared solution norm, and a single folded row opening together.

Proof size grows as O(√n) (~203 KiB at 223, ~532 KiB at 226), verifier RAM stays flat at ~8–10 MiB, and passing certifies a joint 2-norm backward-error upper bound of at most 2−53 (~1.11×10−16) checked in exact integers—without ever transmitting x. Validator spec ↗ · Integer bounds ↗

Clock, identity & verification

No accounts or preregistration are required. Generate an Ed25519 keypair and pass ed25519:<pubkey> as the entrant label to stencil beacon-register; reusing the same private key groups all your runs together on the leaderboard while preventing proof theft.

Submissions include a lightweight Hashcash PoW (~1–3 s) to prevent queue spam and are verified in scheduled batches 5×/day using the unmodified C stencil verify-beacon binary against pinned Sigstore Public Good trust roots. Every verified proof.bin and dataset export is downloadable for independent offline replay.
